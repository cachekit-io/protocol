### Conformance — intent-presets requirement ids

- **Requirement ids**: every MUST and MUST NOT in [intent-presets.md](spec/intent-presets.md)
  (`PRE-1` to `PRE-55`) now carries an id, indexed in
  [conformance/requirements.json](conformance/requirements.json) and reported per SDK in
  [conformance/coverage.md](conformance/coverage.md). No normative text changed;
  `tools/conformance.py strip` removes the ids. Six keywords, in five places, name the word
  rather than state a rule, so they are marked not-a-requirement: the subtitle's "what a
  preset name MUST configure", the two in the sentence that says the keywords are used as in
  RFC 2119, "fixes no MUST on integrity" in Integrity Posture, and the two under Design
  Decisions that explain it.
- **What the new mappings show**: the spec has no fixture of its own, by design, so 37 of
  the 55 map to nothing and the other 18 borrow vectors, each with a gap. The hex master-key
  rules map to every vector outside the draft interop-v2 profile that derives a key from
  one (`interop-mode.json`'s `interop_encryption_roundtrip` and `encryption.json`); every
  such key is exactly 32 bytes of all-digit hex that repeats one nonzero byte, so a preset
  that rejects hex letters, or mishandles their case or a leading zero byte, passes. The
  default-tenant rules map to `encryption.json`'s `default_tenant_interop`, and the rule
  that one resolved tenant feeds both key derivation and the AAD also maps to the vectors
  sealed under a named tenant. The activation rules map to stored entries a test can plant:
  "every operation encrypts" to the two encrypted interop entries and to the plaintext
  interop entry `issue_example_object`, which an encrypting client must refuse, and "the
  master key's presence must not turn encryption on" to that plaintext entry, for the read
  path only. None of them reaches a preset unless an SDK's test drives it through one. All
  55 bind SDKs; 17 bind only the SDKs with the feature they govern, or the languages they
  name: cachekit-py's migration rules and SDK-local presets, cachekit-rs's environment
  constructors and checksum-less container, the raw-bytes key rule (cachekit-py and
  cachekit-rs) and the `encrypted` alias ban (Python and TypeScript). No vector holds a key
  to reject, a preset's TTL or posture, or a construction error, and none pins what an
  SDK's encrypted write stores or what it keeps in L1.
