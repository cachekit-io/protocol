### Encryption — the AAD's `cache_key` is the key exactly as the client hands it to storage

- The [`cache_key` AAD field](spec/encryption.md#additional-authenticated-data-aad) is now
  defined as the full key exactly as the client hands it to storage: after every prefix
  added on the client side, whether an SDK namespace (for example a Rust client's
  `.namespace()`) or a key prefix the backend or its client library prepends (for example
  an ioredis or memjs `keyPrefix`). Scoping a server applies after it receives the key,
  such as the SaaS's per-tenant storage, is not part of it. Ciphertext moved to another
  namespace or prefix under the same master key and tenant must fail authentication.
  Un-namespaced, un-prefixed and interop-mode keys are unchanged: interop mode already
  rejects both prefixes.
- The [no-retry rule](spec/encryption.md#additional-authenticated-data-aad) now names a
  different `cache_key` form, such as the key without its namespace or key prefix, among
  the AAD inputs a reader MUST NOT retry with.
- **Breaking for encrypted entries written under a namespace or key prefix by an SDK that
  bound the key without it:** once that SDK follows this definition, those entries no
  longer authenticate. Delete them, or move to a fresh namespace or prefix, rather than
  waiting out their TTL. A reader does not fall back to the old key form.
