### Wire format — ByteStorage reject vectors (LAB-6508)

- [`test-vectors/wire-format.json`](test-vectors/wire-format.json) **1.3.0** adds
  a `reject_vectors` group: five hex-pinned canonical `bin` envelopes that readers
  MUST reject, each naming in `reject_step` the Retrieve Flow step that rejects it.
  They cover the `original_size` cap (step 4), zero-length `compressed_data` and a
  ratio one byte past 1000:1 (step 5), a declared size one byte larger than the
  decoded output (step 6, or step 9 where the decoder returns a short output), and a
  checksum mismatch (step 8). Until now every vector in the file was an accept
  vector, and a reader with none of these checks passed all of them. The 14
  hex-pinned vectors and the constructed vector are unchanged.
- [`spec/wire-format.md` → Reject vectors](spec/wire-format.md#reject-vectors) is a
  new section. It says what a reader missing each bound does with its vector, and
  adds a MUST: for the size-cap, zero-length and ratio vectors, a failed read does
  not show the bound, so an SDK's conformance test asserts that the named step's
  check rejected the envelope, before decompression allocates `original_size`. For
  the length-mismatch and checksum vectors, the rejection alone is enough. It also
  says why steps 1 and 3 have no vector.
- [`tools/wire-format-reference.py`](tools/wire-format-reference.py) pins the
  reject-vector set in code, derives each vector from its legacy base, and checks
  that its reader rejects each one at its named step. Its reader's rejections now
  carry the step number.
- [`tools/test_wire_format_reference.py`](tools/test_wire_format_reference.py)
  drops each bound from the reference reader in turn and checks that only that
  bound's vector changes outcome, across every vector in the file.
- **For SDKs that vendor `wire-format.json`:** re-vendoring 1.3.0 changes the file's
  sha256. Loaders that read only `vectors` keep passing.
